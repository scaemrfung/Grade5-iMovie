Unit goals

  • Plan a clear story or message for an audience
  • Record and import media using the MacBook camera
  • Edit clips; add titles, transitions, music, and voiceovers
  • Control pacing with Ken Burns, speed, and freeze frames
  • Export a polished video ready for YouTube and Instagram

  • Headphones (strongly recommended)
  • Good lighting facing students when they record

  • Use only iMovie’s built-in music and sound effects
  • Keep clips short and purposeful

Fourteen detailed lesson plans

Each lesson is 30–45 minutes. Expand a lesson for full objectives, warm-up, numbered steps, practice, checks, and teacher tips.

1Welcome to iMovie — Exploring the Interface30–40 min · Interface navigation▼

Objectives

  • Open iMovie and create a new Movie project
  • Name the four main areas of the window
  • Play, pause, zoom, scroll, and undo using the trackpad and keyboard

You will need

  • MacBook with iMovie installed
  • Headphones optional

Warm-up (5 min)

Watch a short example video chosen by the teacher. Discuss: What made it easy to follow? What would you change?

1. Open iMovie and start a project

  1. 1Click the Launchpad icon in the Dock, or press Command + Space to open Spotlight.
  2. 2Type iMovie and press Return.
  3. 3If iMovie asks for Photos or camera permission, click Allow.
  4. 4You should see the Projects browser — a screen of existing movies (or mostly empty if this is the first time).
  5. 5Click Create New (the large plus tile).
  6. 6Choose Movie, not Trailer. Trailer comes later.
  7. 7A blank project opens. This is the editing workspace.

2. Name the four main areas

  1. 1Media Library (left side) — where clips, photos, and audio live before they go on the timeline.
  2. 2Viewer (top right) — the large preview of whatever is under the playhead.
  3. 3Timeline (bottom) — where you build the story from left to right.
  4. 4Toolbar / content tabs — Media, Audio, Titles, Backgrounds, Transitions across the top of the media area.

3. Practice moving around

  1. 1Press the spacebar to play and pause.
  2. 2Two-finger scroll the timeline left and right on the trackpad.
  3. 3Pinch on the trackpad to zoom the timeline in and out.
  4. 4Press Command + Z to undo. This is the most important shortcut in the unit.
  5. 5Click the project name at the top and rename it Practice1_YourName. iMovie saves automatically.

Practice

  • Point to and name each of the four areas for a partner.
  • Play, pause, zoom, and undo once each.

Check for understanding

  • Can the student open iMovie and create a Movie project without help?
  • Can they name the four main areas?
  • Can they play, pause, and undo?
Teacher tip: Circulate and have students point to the Media Library, Viewer, Timeline, and toolbar before leaving the demo area. Fix login and permission pop-ups in this lesson so later lessons stay on track.
2Creating a Project, Importing, and Recording35–45 min · Import + MacBook camera▼

Objectives

  • Import photos or videos from Photos or Finder
  • Record a short clip with the built-in FaceTime HD camera
  • Drag clips onto the timeline in a clear order

You will need

  • MacBook with camera and microphone access allowed
  • Good light on the student’s face

Warm-up (3–5 min)

Show one well-framed face shot and one poorly lit shot. Ask: Which is easier to watch, and why?

1. Create or open the project

  1. 1Open iMovie → Create New → Movie (or open your Lesson 1 project).
  2. 2Name it ImportPractice_YourName.

2. Import existing photos or videos

  1. 1Click the Import Media button (downward arrow) above the media browser.
  2. 2Browse to Photos, Desktop, or a class folder the teacher provided.
  3. 3Select 2–3 files → Import Selected. Clips appear in Project Media.

3. Record with the MacBook camera

  1. 1Click Import Media again. Under Cameras, click FaceTime HD Camera.
  2. 2Allow camera and microphone access if macOS asks.
  3. 3Sit about an arm’s length from the laptop. Face a window or lamp. Look at the camera lens, not the screen.
  4. 4Click the red Record button. Say a short sentence (for example, “Hi, my name is ___.”). Click Record again to stop.
  5. 5Keep takes under about 10 seconds. You will trim later.

4. Build a first sequence

  1. 1Drag clips from Project Media onto the timeline.
  2. 2Arrange them left to right in a sensible order.
  3. 3Press spacebar and watch the whole sequence once.

Practice

  • Record one extra take with better lighting or a clearer voice.
  • Delete the weaker take from the timeline (not necessarily from the library).

Check for understanding

  • Is at least one self-recorded clip on the timeline?
  • Is the face reasonably well lit?
  • Can the student import media without help?
Teacher tip: Sit an arm’s length from the MacBook. Face a window or lamp. Backlight makes faces dark. If the preview is black, check System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera and Microphone.
3Finding and Saving Images from the Internet30–40 min · Download, organize, import▼

Objectives

  • Find appropriate images from teacher-approved websites
  • Save images to a clear folder on the MacBook
  • Import saved images into an iMovie project
  • Practice simple digital citizenship (permission, attribution, safe search)

You will need

  • MacBook with Safari (or Chrome) and iMovie
  • Teacher-approved image sites (list on the board)
  • An iMovie project open (or create ImagePractice_YourName)

Warm-up (5 min)

Show two images on the projector: one the teacher owns or has permission to use, and one that is a famous logo or movie character. Ask: Which one is safer to put in a school video we might share? Why?

Class rule for this unit: Only use (1) photos you took, (2) images the teacher provides, or (3) images from the approved free sites listed in class. When you use a web image, add a short credit in your end titles (example: “Photo: Pixabay”).

1. Know where you are allowed to search

  1. 1Use only sites your teacher lists. Common classroom choices: Pixabay, Unsplash, Wikimedia Commons, or a folder the teacher shared.
  2. 2Do not download random images from social media, shopping sites, or search results that show copyrighted characters, logos, or watermarks.
  3. 3If you are unsure, ask before you save.

2. Create a project folder on the MacBook

  1. 1Click the Finder icon in the Dock.
  2. 2In the sidebar, click Desktop (or Documents if the teacher prefers).
  3. 3From the menu bar choose File → New Folder (or press Shift + Command + N).
  4. 4Name the folder iMovieImages_YourName. Press Return.
  5. 5Keep this folder for the whole unit. All web images for iMovie go here first — not scattered in Downloads.

3. Find and save an image (Safari)

  1. 1Open Safari.
  2. 2Go to a teacher-approved site (type the address carefully, or use a link the teacher shared).
  3. 3Search for a simple topic you need (example: “mountain”, “bicycle”, “library”). Prefer clear photos, not memes or text-heavy graphics.
  4. 4Click the image to open a larger view when the site allows it.
  5. 5Control-click (or right-click with a mouse) on the image.
  6. 6Choose Save Image As… (wording may be “Download Linked File As…” on some pages).
  7. 7In the save dialog, navigate to your iMovieImages_YourName folder.
  8. 8Give the file a clear name before saving, for example mountain_trail.jpg or bike_helmet.png. Avoid names like IMG_4821 or download.
  9. 9Click Save.

4. Check the file in Finder

  1. 1Open Finder and open your iMovieImages_YourName folder.
  2. 2Confirm the image is there. Click it once and press the spacebar for Quick Look.
  3. 3If the file is tiny, blurry, or not the image you wanted, delete it and try again from the website.
  4. 4Save 2–4 images maximum for today’s practice so the folder stays organized.

5. Import the images into iMovie

  1. 1Open iMovie and open your practice project (or Create New → Movie named ImagePractice_YourName).
  2. 2Click the Import Media button (downward arrow).
  3. 3Navigate to Desktop (or Documents) → iMovieImages_YourName.
  4. 4Select the images you need → click Import Selected.
  5. 5The photos appear in Project Media. Drag one onto the timeline to confirm it works.
  6. 6Optional: leave the photo on the timeline for Lesson 8 (Ken Burns). For today, importing successfully is the goal.

6. Credit the source (good habit)

  1. 1On paper or in Notes, write the site name for each image you saved (example: Pixabay — mountain trail).
  2. 2When you finish a real project, add a short end title such as: Photos: Pixabay and my own camera.
  3. 3You do not need a full bibliography in Grade 5, but you do need honest credit and approved sources.

Practice

  • Create the folder iMovieImages_YourName.
  • Save 2 appropriate images from an approved site with clear file names.
  • Import both into iMovie and place one on the timeline.
  • Write the source name for each image.

Check for understanding

  • Can the student save an image into their named folder (not a mystery Downloads pile)?
  • Can they import that image into iMovie without help?
  • Can they name one rule about which images are allowed?
Teacher tip: Post 2–3 approved URLs on the board before the lesson. For Grade 5, avoid open Google Image search unless you pre-teach Tools → Usage rights and supervise closely. If bandwidth or filtering is an issue, provide a shared class folder of pre-cleared images and still teach the Save → Folder → Import path using those files. Watch for students saving entire web pages by mistake — they need the image file (.jpg / .png), not an .html file.
4Basic Editing — Trim, Split, Delete, Rearrange35–45 min · Core timeline skills▼

Objectives

  • Trim clip ends with the yellow handles
  • Split a clip and delete the unwanted middle piece
  • Rearrange clips so the story is short and clear

Warm-up (3 min)

Show a 20-second clip with a long shaky start. Ask: What would you cut first?

1. Select and trim

  1. 1Click a clip on the timeline so a yellow border appears.
  2. 2Drag the yellow handles at either end inward to hide shaky starts or long endings.
  3. 3Zoom the timeline in (pinch) if the handles are hard to grab.
  4. 4Play the trimmed clip to check the result.

2. Split a clip

  1. 1Place the playhead on the first frame of a mistake (a cough, a long “um,” or a false start).
  2. 2Click the Split button (scissors) or press Command + B.
  3. 3Move the playhead to the end of the mistake and split again.
  4. 4Select the middle piece and press Delete.

3. Rearrange and clean

  1. 1Drag clips left or right on the timeline to change order.
  2. 2Play the whole sequence. Goal: a clean 20–40 second practice cut.
  3. 3If you make a mistake, press Command + Z.

Practice

  • Trim every clip on your timeline.
  • Remove at least one unwanted middle section with split + delete.
  • Partner check: give one “keep” and one “improve.”

Check for understanding

  • Are shaky starts gone?
  • Can the student explain the difference between trim and split?
  • Is the practice sequence 20–40 seconds?
Teacher tip: Short and clear beats long and messy. Zoom in before fine trims. Model Command + Z early and often so students are not afraid to try.
5Transitions and Smooth Flow30–40 min · Visual flow▼

Objectives

  • Add, change, and delete transitions between clips
  • Choose simple transitions that help the story instead of decorating every cut
  • Adjust transition duration so cuts feel natural

Warm-up (3–5 min)

Show two versions of the same cut: a hard cut and a Cross Dissolve. Ask which feels smoother for a calm story, and when a hard cut might be better.

1. Open the Transitions browser

  1. 1Click the Transitions tab above the media browser.
  2. 2Hover over styles to preview them in the Viewer.
  3. 3Find simple options first: Cross Dissolve, Fade to Black, Fade to White.

2. Add a transition

  1. 1Drag Cross Dissolve into the gap between two clips on the timeline. A small icon appears in the gap.
  2. 2Play across the cut. Notice how one shot blends into the next.
  3. 3Double-click the transition icon. Set duration to about 0.5–1.5 seconds. Click Apply.

3. Change or remove a transition

  1. 1To change style: drag a different transition on top of the existing icon.
  2. 2To remove: click the transition icon once and press Delete.
  3. 3Add only 2–4 transitions in the whole project. Prefer Cross Dissolve or Fade.
  4. 4Avoid using a different flashy transition on every cut — it usually distracts.

Practice

  • Add two transitions. Delete one and replace it with a simpler choice.
  • Watch the whole timeline and decide whether each transition helps the story.

Check for understanding

  • Can the student add a transition between two clips?
  • Can they change the duration?
  • Do they understand that fewer, simpler transitions are usually better?
Teacher tip: A hard cut is often the best choice. Praise restraint. Model one busy “every-cut transition” version and one calm version so students can hear and see the difference.
6Titles, Text, and Captions35–45 min · On-screen text▼

Objectives

  • Add an opening title, an optional name caption, and end text
  • Edit title text in the Viewer and keep it on screen long enough to read (about 3–5 seconds)
  • Choose readable, high-contrast title styles

Warm-up (3 min)

Show a title that disappears after one second. Ask: Could you read that? How long should a title stay on screen?

1. Open the Titles browser

  1. 1Click the Titles tab above the media browser.
  2. 2Browse styles such as Centered, Lower Third, and Standard. Prefer simple styles for classroom work.

2. Add and edit an opening title

  1. 1Drag Centered (or a similar simple style) to the start of the timeline. A purple title bar appears.
  2. 2Click the purple title bar, then click the text in the Viewer.
  3. 3Type All About Me – Your Name (or the title for today’s practice).
  4. 4Drag the ends of the purple bar so the title stays on screen for 3–5 seconds.

3. Add a caption and end text

  1. 1Optional: drag Lower Third onto a talking clip and type the student’s name.
  2. 2Add end text such as Thanks for watching or Created by Your Name.
  3. 3Check spelling carefully.
  4. 4Have a partner read every title aloud without pausing the video. If they miss a word, lengthen the title or enlarge the text.

Practice

  • Create opening title, optional lower third, and end title.
  • Partner check: can they read every word comfortably?

Check for understanding

  • Is there a clear opening title with the student’s name?
  • Do titles stay on screen about 3–5 seconds?
  • Are titles high-contrast and easy to read?
Teacher tip: Prefer high-contrast titles (light on dark or dark on light). Avoid tiny text or busy backgrounds behind words. Spelling counts — build a quick partner-read step into every title lesson.
7Music, Sound Effects, and Volume35–45 min · Audio foundation▼

Objectives

  • Add one music bed and one or two sound effects from iMovie’s built-in library
  • Lower music under speech and fade audio in and out
  • Use headphones to judge a balanced mix

Warm-up (3–5 min)

Play a clip with music that drowns the voice. Ask what is wrong and how you would fix it.

1. Browse and preview audio

  1. 1Put on headphones. Click the Audio tab above the media browser.
  2. 2Browse Theme Music and Sound Effects.
  3. 3Click tracks to preview them before dragging anything to the timeline.

2. Add background music

  1. 1Drag one music bed onto the timeline under the video.
  2. 2Shorten the music so it matches the movie length by dragging the ends.
  3. 3Hover near the start or end of the music clip until fade handles appear. Drag them for smooth fades.
  4. 4Select the music clip and lower the volume until any talking is clearly louder.

3. Add sound effects

  1. 1Drag 1–2 sound effects to the exact moment they should play (a whoosh on a title, a click on a finished step).
  2. 2Keep effects short and purposeful.
  3. 3Do not use outside music unless the teacher supplies approved files. Stick to iMovie’s library.

Practice

  • Add music + one effect.
  • Partner listens on headphones and checks that voice wins over music.

Check for understanding

  • Is there one music bed that fades in and out?
  • Is the voice (if present) louder than the music?
  • Are sound effects timed to a clear moment?
Teacher tip: Laptop speakers hide mix problems. Require headphones for this lesson. Music supports the video; it never overpowers voices. If students struggle to find fade handles, zoom the timeline in.
8Voiceovers and Narration35–45 min · MacBook microphone▼

Objectives

  • Write a short script and record a voiceover with the MacBook microphone
  • Replace a bad take and balance voice against music
  • Speak clearly, a little slower than normal conversation

Warm-up (3–5 min)

Model one clear, slow sentence vs one rushed, quiet sentence. Ask which is easier to understand.

1. Write and practice the words

  1. 1Write 2–4 short sentences on paper or in Notes.
  2. 2Practice once out loud. Fix any tongue-twisters before recording.

2. Record the voiceover

  1. 1Place the playhead where narration should start.
  2. 2Click the microphone / Record Voiceover button under the Viewer.
  3. 3If there is a mute-project option, use it so old sound does not leak into the new take.
  4. 4Click Record, wait for the countdown, and speak clearly after it ends.
  5. 5Click Record again to stop. A green voiceover track appears under the video.

3. Check and balance

  1. 1Listen on headphones. If the take is muddy, select the green track, press Delete, and record again.
  2. 2Do not keep the first take out of habit.
  3. 3Raise the green voiceover so words are easy to hear. Keep music quieter than the voice.

Practice

  • Record two takes of the same lines. Keep the clearer one.
  • Partner feedback: volume, clarity, and pace.

Check for understanding

  • Is there a green voiceover track on the timeline?
  • Is the voice clearer than any background music?
  • Did the student re-record at least once if needed?
Teacher tip: Quiet corner or hallway helps. Speak a little slower than normal and smile — it makes the voice friendlier. Allow hallway passes if the classroom is loud. Check that microphone permission is allowed in System Settings if recording fails.
9Photos and the Ken Burns Effect30–40 min · Still images in motion▼

Objectives

  • Add photos to a video timeline
  • Apply a slow Ken Burns zoom or pan so still images feel alive
  • Keep movement gentle so the audience can still see the photo

Warm-up (3 min)

Show a still photo held static for 5 seconds, then the same photo with a slow Ken Burns zoom. Ask which holds attention better and why.

1. Add a photo to the timeline

  1. 1Import a photo (Import Media or from Photos).
  2. 2Drag the photo onto the timeline where you want it.
  3. 3Adjust the photo’s length to about 4–6 seconds by dragging its ends.

2. Turn on Ken Burns

  1. 1Select the photo on the timeline.
  2. 2Click Cropping above the Viewer (looks like overlapping corners).
  3. 3Choose Ken Burns.
  4. 4Two rectangles appear: Start (green) and End (red).

3. Set the movement

  1. 1Resize and move the Start rectangle to show how the photo should look at the beginning.
  2. 2Resize and move the End rectangle to show how it should look at the end.
  3. 3A gentle zoom in on an important detail, or a slow pull back, works well.
  4. 4Click Apply. Play the photo. If the motion feels dizzy, make the Start and End frames closer together.
  5. 5Keep movement slow. The audience should still be able to see what the photo shows.

Practice

  • Apply Ken Burns to one or two photos.
  • Partner feedback: too fast, too slow, or just right?

Check for understanding

  • Is at least one photo on the timeline with Ken Burns applied?
  • Is the motion slow enough to follow?
  • Can the student explain Start vs End frames?
Teacher tip: Good use is a slow zoom in on a detail or a slow reveal of the whole scene. Avoid wild pans. If students cannot find Cropping, have them select the photo first — the button only appears for the selected clip.
10Video Effects, Filters, and Simple Color30–40 min · Visual mood▼

Objectives

  • Preview clip filters and simple color adjustments
  • Keep or remove an effect based on whether it helps the audience
  • Use effects sparingly (one or two clips, not the whole project)

Warm-up (3–5 min)

Show the same clip with no filter, a mild warm look, and a heavy stylized filter. Discuss: When does an effect help, and when does it distract?

1. Open the effect controls

  1. 1Select a video clip on the timeline.
  2. 2Open Clip Filter (or color / adjustment controls) above the Viewer. Look for icons that suggest filters or color wheels.

2. Try and judge effects

  1. 1Try a few filters. After each one, ask: Does this help the feeling of the story?
  2. 2Keep a filter on one or two clips only, or choose None if it distracts.
  3. 3Optional: small brightness or contrast tweaks if a face is a little dark — do not crush the image.
  4. 4Play the clip in context with the shots before and after. An effect that looks cool alone may feel odd in the full sequence.

3. Decide and clean up

  1. 1Remove any effect that does not support the story.
  2. 2Write one sentence: “I kept / removed the effect because ___.”

Practice

  • Apply one filter, then remove it. Decide which version is clearer for a classmate.
  • Share one intentional choice with a partner.

Check for understanding

  • Can the student apply and remove a filter?
  • Do they have a reason for keeping or discarding it?
  • Are effects limited to one or two clips?
Teacher tip: Less is almost always more professional. Praise students who choose no filter on purpose. The skill is judgment, not stacking effects.
11Speed Changes and Freeze Frames35–45 min · Pacing control▼

Objectives

  • Speed up or slow down a clip on purpose
  • Create a freeze frame on an important moment
  • Use speed only where it serves the story

Warm-up (3–5 min)

Show one slow-motion highlight and one rushed talking clip. Discuss when speed helps and when it hurts.

1. Change clip speed

  1. 1Select a clip that shows action (not essential talking, unless it is a clear joke).
  2. 2Open the Speed controls above the Viewer (speedometer or tortoise/hare style icon).
  3. 3Choose Slow for drama or Fast for energy. Start mild (about 50% or 2x).
  4. 4Play the result. Adjust if it feels too extreme.

2. Create a freeze frame

  1. 1Move the playhead to the exact frame you want to freeze.
  2. 2Open Speed → choose Freeze Frame.
  3. 3iMovie creates a still that stays on screen. Hold the freeze for about 1–3 seconds.
  4. 4Optional: place a short title over the freeze for emphasis.

3. Use speed purposefully

  1. 1Use one speed change or freeze in a practice project — not every clip.
  2. 2Watch the whole sequence. Does the timing still feel natural?
  3. 3Do not speed talking unless the joke still makes sense.

Practice

  • Apply one Slow or Fast change to an action clip.
  • Create one freeze frame on a key moment.
  • Partner check: does the pacing help or distract?

Check for understanding

  • Can the student change speed and create a freeze frame?
  • Is speed used on purpose, not on every clip?
  • Does talking remain understandable?
Teacher tip: Pacing serves the story. Ban “speed everything” experiments for the graded project. A single well-chosen freeze or slow-motion beat is more impressive than constant speed changes.
12iMovie Trailers and Structured Storytelling35–45 min · Templates and story structure▼

Objectives

  • Create a Trailer project from a template
  • Fill Outline and Storyboard with original media and text (no leftover sample names)
  • Understand beginning (hook), middle, and ending structure

Warm-up (5 min)

Discuss beginning (hook), middle (action or information), and ending (strong final image). Name one movie trailer students know and identify those three parts.

1. Start a Trailer project

  1. 1Click Projects (top left) → Create New → Trailer.
  2. 2Preview two kid-friendly templates. Pick the mood that matches your idea — not only the flashiest one.
  3. 3Click Create.

2. Fill the Outline

  1. 1Click the Outline tab.
  2. 2Replace every placeholder with your own title, cast names, and credits.
  3. 3No leftover “Studio” or sample names should remain.

3. Fill the Storyboard

  1. 1Click the Storyboard tab.
  2. 2Each placeholder shows what kind of shot is needed (Title, Action, Emotion, etc.).
  3. 3Drag your own clips or photos into every required box.
  4. 4Play Trailer. Swap any shot that does not match the caption or mood.
  5. 5If the template already has music, do not stack a second loud track on top.

Practice

  • Complete a short practice trailer with 4–6 of your own clips or photos.
  • Partner check: are all Outline placeholders replaced?

Check for understanding

  • Is every Outline field original (no sample names)?
  • Is every Storyboard slot filled with the student’s media?
  • Can the student name hook, middle, and ending in their trailer?
Teacher tip: Trailers teach structure: hook, middle, strong final image. Check Outline carefully before calling the work done — leftover placeholder text is the most common miss. Preview templates with the class so students choose mood, not just “coolest graphics.”
13Polishing, Organizing, and Optional Overlays35–45 min · Final refinements▼

Objectives

  • Watch the whole film critically and fix pacing, titles, and audio
  • Use a polishing checklist before export day
  • Optionally try Picture-in-Picture

Warm-up (3 min)

Explain “cold watch”: play once with no pausing, then write fixes. Model writing three concrete fixes from a sample project.

1. Cold watch

  1. 1Put on headphones. Press spacebar and watch from beginning to end with no pausing.
  2. 2Write three specific fixes (example: “title too short,” “music too loud under voice,” “last shot cuts off”).

2. Polishing checklist

  1. 1Titles: readable, 3–5 seconds, spelled correctly, no leftover placeholders.
  2. 2Audio: voice clear on headphones, music under speech, fades at the ends.
  3. 3Picture: no shaky starts, long empty pauses, or leftover mistakes.
  4. 4Story: clear beginning, middle, and ending. Strong last shot or end title.
  5. 5Fix each note from the cold watch using trim, split, volume, or titles.

3. Optional: Picture-in-Picture

  1. 1Drag a second clip above the main video track.
  2. 2Select it → Video Overlay Settings → Picture in Picture.
  3. 3Resize and move the inset so it does not cover important faces or text.
  4. 4Use PiP only if it helps (for example, a demo in the corner while you narrate).

Practice

  • Complete the cold watch and checklist on your current project.
  • Peer review: two stars and one wish.

Check for understanding

  • Did the student complete a cold watch and write three fixes?
  • Are titles, audio, and ending cleaned up?
  • If PiP was used, does it help rather than hide important content?
Teacher tip: Require the written three-fix list before students claim they are “done.” Peer review works best with sentence stems: “One thing that worked well was… One thing to improve is…”
14Exporting, Sharing, Reflecting, and Celebration30–45 min · Social-media ready export▼

Objectives

  • Export a 1080p file with a clear name
  • Explain what makes the video ready for YouTube or Instagram
  • Reflect on learning and celebrate finished work

Warm-up (3 min)

Show the Share menu and the difference between a project inside iMovie and a finished .mp4 file in Finder. Emphasize: the export is the copy that counts.

1. Final check

  1. 1Watch the entire project one last time on headphones.
  2. 2Confirm titles, audio balance, privacy (permission for faces), and a strong last frame.

2. Export the video

  1. 1Click Share (top right) → File.
  2. 2Set Resolution to 1080p. Leave Quality on High.
  3. 3Name the file clearly, e.g. Alex_FinalShowcase_2026.mp4.
  4. 4Save where the teacher asked (class folder, Desktop, etc.).
  5. 5Wait for export to finish. Open the file in QuickTime to confirm it plays outside iMovie.

3. Reflect

  1. 1Write three lines: What makes my video ready to share online? One editing skill I am proud of. One thing I would do differently next time.
  2. 2Optional class viewing or gallery walk of strong examples (only with student permission).

Practice

  • Export the Final Showcase (and earlier projects if needed) as 1080p files.
  • Complete the three-line reflection.

Check for understanding

  • Does a .mp4 file exist outside iMovie with a clear name?
  • Does it open and play in QuickTime?
  • Can the student explain one reason the video is social-media ready?
Teacher tip: Under 90 seconds often works best for Instagram. A strong opening in the first 5–10 seconds matters on every platform. Never post a classmate’s face without permission. YouTube can be longer, but the same strong opening still matters. Collect reflections before celebration so quiet students still process the learning.

Four student projects

Each project includes planning, a walkthrough, and a clear 1–4 rubric. Score every category, then add the numbers for a total.

Project 1 · Due after Lessons 1–6 · 30–60 seconds

All About Me

A personal introduction using mostly MacBook-camera clips, basic editing, titles, and transitions.

Audience: Classmates and family. Keep it friendly, true, and easy to follow.

Must include

  • 4–7 short clips or photos
  • Opening title with the student’s name
  • 2–3 purposeful transitions
  • Clear beginning, middle, and end

Plan first

  1. 1Write three beats: Who I am · One thing I love · One thing I want people to know.
  2. 2List shots: face intro, object or hobby, optional photo, closing smile.
  3. 3Keep each recorded shot under 10 seconds.

Walkthrough

  1. 1Create New → Movie. Name FirstName_AllAboutMe.
  2. 2Record 3 short talking clips with FaceTime HD Camera. Import extras if wanted.
  3. 3Arrange intro · details · ending. Trim shaky starts. Target 30–60 seconds.
  4. 4Add opening title, optional lower third, end text. Add 2–3 transitions.
  5. 5Watch once without pausing. Fix spelling, timing, and messy cuts.
  6. 6Export: Share → File → 1080p → FirstName_AllAboutMe.mp4.

How to score this project

Scale: 1 Beginning · 2 Developing · 3 Proficient · 4 Extending

Categories (4): Story · Editing · Titles · Export

Total: add the four scores → /16

Proficient: total 12 or higher, and a classmate can tell who the student is after one watch.

Rubric

Story — 1 Beginning

Clips feel random. No clear beginning or ending.

Story — 2 Developing

There is an intro, but the middle jumps or the ending cuts off suddenly.

Story — 3 Proficient

Beginning, middle, and end are clear. Length is 30–60 seconds.

Story — 4 Extending

Three planned beats feel personal. The ending lands on purpose.

Editing — 1 Beginning

Shaky starts, long pauses, and leftover mistakes remain.

Editing — 2 Developing

Some trims are done, but several clips are still messy.

Editing — 3 Proficient

Clips are trimmed. 2–3 transitions are used with care.

Editing — 4 Extending

Pacing is tight. Transitions help the story and never decorate every cut.

Titles — 1 Beginning

Missing title, or text is too fast or hard to read.

Titles — 2 Developing

Title exists, but spelling, timing, or contrast needs work.

Titles — 3 Proficient

Opening title includes the name. Text is readable for 3–5 seconds.

Titles — 4 Extending

Opening, optional caption, and end text are clear, spelled correctly, and well timed.

Export — 1 Beginning

No finished file, or only the iMovie project was turned in.

Export — 2 Developing

A file exists, but the name is unclear or quality is below 1080p without a reason.

Export — 3 Proficient

1080p export with a clear name such as FirstName_AllAboutMe.mp4.

Export — 4 Extending

Clean 1080p file, clear name, and the student can open it outside iMovie to show it works.

Student self-check

  • □ My name is on the opening title
  • □ A partner can read every title without pausing
  • □ Ums and shaky starts are cut
  • □ Video is 30–60 seconds
  • □ I exported a 1080p file with a clear name

Project 2 · Due after Lessons 7–10 · 45–90 seconds

How-To or A Day in My Life

Instructional or narrative piece with voiceover or on-camera speaking and balanced music.

Audience: Someone who does not already know the steps or the student’s day. The video should teach or show clearly.

Must include

  • Voiceover or on-camera speaking recorded on the MacBook
  • Background music at a proper level
  • Titles and transitions
  • At least one sound effect

Plan first

  1. 1Choose How-To (4–7 steps) or A Day in My Life (morning → school → highlight → end).
  2. 2Write short commands and matching narration for each step.
  3. 3Mark where one sound effect will land.

Walkthrough

  1. 1Create Movie named FirstName_HowTo or FirstName_DayInMyLife.
  2. 2One clip per step. Trim hard. Target 45–90 seconds.
  3. 3Record voiceover. Re-record muddy takes. Voice louder than music.
  4. 4Add iMovie music (ducked), one sound effect, titles, 2–4 transitions.
  5. 5Optional Ken Burns on stills. Export 1080p with a clear file name.

How to score this project

Scale: 1 Beginning · 2 Developing · 3 Proficient · 4 Extending

Categories (5): Sequence · Voice · Music & sound effects · Titles & editing · Export

Total: add the five scores → /20

Proficient: total 15 or higher, and a first-time viewer can follow the steps or the day. Voice is louder than music.

Rubric

Sequence — 1 Beginning

Order is confusing. Steps are missing or jumpy.

Sequence — 2 Developing

Most steps are there, but one part is hard to follow.

Sequence — 3 Proficient

Order is clear. A viewer can follow the how-to or the day.

Sequence — 4 Extending

Tight, complete sequence. Easy to teach back after one watch.

Voice — 1 Beginning

Voice is missing, too quiet, or mostly unclear.

Voice — 2 Developing

Voice is present, but rushed, uneven, or partly hard to hear.

Voice — 3 Proficient

Voice is clear, paced, and matches the picture.

Voice — 4 Extending

Voice is confident, well paced, and easy for a classmate to understand on first listen.

Music & SFX — 1 Beginning

Music overpowers speech, or required audio is missing.

Music & SFX — 2 Developing

Music is present, but balance or timing still needs work.

Music & SFX — 3 Proficient

Music sits under the voice. At least one sound effect is timed to a clear moment.

Music & SFX — 4 Extending

Mix is balanced with fades. Sound effect feels planned, not random.

Titles & editing — 1 Beginning

Little editing. Titles missing or unreadable.

Titles & editing — 2 Developing

Some trims and a title, but several weak spots remain.

Titles & editing — 3 Proficient

Required titles are present. Clips are trimmed. Transitions are controlled.

Titles & editing — 4 Extending

Clean trims, readable titles, and purposeful transitions throughout.

Export — 1 Beginning

No finished file turned in.

Export — 2 Developing

File exists, but naming or quality is incomplete.

Export — 3 Proficient

1080p export with a clear project name.

Export — 4 Extending

Clean 1080p file, clear name, and confirmed playback outside iMovie.

Student self-check

  • □ Someone new could follow my steps or my day
  • □ My voice is louder than the music on headphones
  • □ I included at least one sound effect on purpose
  • □ Titles are readable for 3–5 seconds
  • □ I exported a 1080p file with a clear name

Project 3 · Due after Lessons 11–13 · 60–90 seconds

Mini Story or Trailer-Style Piece

Creative or informative video showing pacing control and visual interest.

Audience: Classmates. Aim for a strong first 5 seconds and a chosen last shot.

Must include

  • Varied pacing (normal + speed change or freeze), or a complete Trailer structure
  • Titles, music or voice, and transitions
  • Ken Burns, a purposeful effect, or full Trailer placeholders replaced with original media

Plan first

  1. 1Pick Mini Story (Movie) or Trailer template.
  2. 2Write Hook (first 5 seconds), Middle (problem or information), Ending (image or line that sticks).
  3. 3Circle one moment that will be slow-motion, fast, or frozen — or plan every Trailer box.

Walkthrough

  1. 1Create the project. Name it FirstName_Story or FirstName_Trailer.
  2. 2Build Hook → Middle → Ending. Add one Slow/Fast or Freeze Frame, or complete every Trailer box with original media.
  3. 3Replace all template placeholders. Clean titles and audio.
  4. 4Export 1080p with a clear file name.

Pacing / craft — 3 Proficient

Audio — 3 Proficient

Music or voice supports the picture without fighting it.

Project 4 · Due at end of unit · 60–90 seconds

Final Showcase

Student choice: short documentary, original story, positive-habit commercial, or expanded trailer. Polished and ready for YouTube or Instagram.

Audience: A stranger should understand the purpose within about 5 seconds. Treat this as the unit’s public-ready piece.

Must include

  • Most unit skills (picture, titles, audio, plus one advanced choice)
  • Clean 1080p export with a clear file name
  • Short reflection (3 lines)
  • Optional pairs with defined roles (director, editor, talent)

Walkthrough

  1. 1Create FirstName_Showcase. Rough cut: hook → middle → ending.
  2. 2Add titles, audio, and one advanced skill (Ken Burns, speed/freeze, Trailer, or Picture-in-Picture).
  3. 3Cold watch on headphones. Fix spelling, volume, privacy, and the last frame.
  4. 4Peer review (two stars + one wish). Export 1080p. Write the 3-line reflection.

Teacher notes

Approved images from the internet

  • Teach Lesson 3 before students need stills for Projects 2–4.
  • Post 2–3 approved free-image sites; avoid unsupervised Google Image search in Grade 5.
  • Require a named folder (iMovieImages_YourName) so files are not lost in Downloads.
  • End credits may be simple: “Photos: Pixabay and my own camera.”
  • If the network blocks downloads, use a shared class folder of pre-cleared images and still practice Import Media.

Camera and recording

  • Practice the built-in camera in Lesson 2 so framing is not new during projects.
  • Have students face a window or lamp. Backlight makes faces dark.
  • Quiet corners or a hallway help voiceovers stay clean.
  • If the preview is black, check System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera and Microphone.

Storage and workflow

  • iMovie libraries grow quickly. Export and delete practice projects on a regular schedule.
  • Require consistent names from day one.
  • Show the Projects button (top left) so students can return to the browser any time.
  • A finished export in Finder is the copy that counts — not only the iMovie project.

Differentiation

  • Provide pre-trimmed media or sentence starters for narration.
  • Offer a quieter recording space.
  • Challenge: Picture-in-Picture, a second speed change, or a slightly longer showcase.
  • Pairs work well on the final project if roles are named (director, editor, talent).

Assessment

  • Formative: circulate, check timelines, listen to partner feedback.
  • Summative: the four projects plus a short reflection on social-media readiness.
  • Score 1–4 on the categories listed in each project.
  • Key final question: Is this video polished and ready to share on YouTube or Instagram?
  • Suggested weighting: Projects 1–3 average 50%, Showcase 40%, daily process 10%.

Export for social platforms

  • Default student export: File, 1080p, High quality, .mp4.
  • Instagram and similar feeds prefer about 60–90 seconds with a strong first 5 seconds.
  • YouTube can be longer, but the same opening still matters.
  • iMovie on Mac exports widescreen 16:9 — correct for YouTube and landscape Instagram posts.
  • Never post a classmate’s face without permission.
